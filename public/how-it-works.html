The complete mechanics

One algorithm. Three positions. No discretion.

Ocean Blue is rules-based, not opinion-based. The algorithm evaluates the same conditions every market day and produces one of three outputs: hold QQQ, hold TLT, or hold cash. Every subscriber gets the same signal.

The daily cycle

What happens every market day

5:30 PM ET

Signal computed

The algorithm pulls today's QQQ and TLT closing prices, runs the v3.5.1 rules, and determines whether the regime changed.

5:35 PM ET

Alert delivered

If a position change is required, every subscriber receives an SMS and email simultaneously. If no change, no message is sent.

Next 9:30 AM ET

You execute

Place the trade in your IRA brokerage account at the next market open. Full rotation, no partial allocations.

The three positions

QQQ (Invesco QQQ Trust)

The Nasdaq-100 ETF. This is the default position when the algorithm detects a bullish regime — price above its 200-day moving average with confirmation, and no bearish trend signals firing.

TLT (iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF)

Used as a defensive rotation when the algorithm exits QQQ AND long-term Treasury bonds are themselves in a bullish regime (TLT above its own 200-day moving average). This historically captures the "flight to safety" trade during equity bear markets.

Cash

Used when the algorithm exits QQQ but TLT is also weak. In an IRA, this is your settlement fund (money market). Earns risk-free interest while waiting for the next equity entry signal.

The signal inputs

The algorithm evaluates several indicators each day. The exact rules and parameters are proprietary, but the high-level components are:

  • Regime filter — price relative to long-term moving average
  • Volume confirmation — accumulation/distribution patterns
  • Trend strength filter — distance from key moving averages
  • Breakout detection — for re-entries after bear markets
  • Whipsaw protection — distance filters that suppress signals near regime boundaries

What the algorithm does NOT use

  • Macroeconomic data (GDP, employment, inflation reports)
  • News headlines or sentiment analysis
  • Earnings reports or analyst opinions
  • Fed announcements or central bank communications
  • Single-stock analysis
  • Options activity or VIX levels

Price and volume data only. The discipline of ignoring everything else is what allows the algorithm to remain consistent across regimes.

Why ~25 trades per year (not more, not fewer)

Over 22 years of backtesting, the algorithm averaged 558 position changes (about 25 per year). Some years had as few as 8 changes; the most active year had ~40 (during 2009-2010 whipsaws). The pattern is heavily concentrated around regime transitions — most days produce no signal at all.
