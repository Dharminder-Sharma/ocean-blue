For self-directed retirement accounts

A strategy designed for your IRA.

Ocean Blue makes ~25 position changes per year. In a taxable account, that frequency creates devastating tax friction. In an IRA, it creates compounding returns.

The tax math

Why this strategy and IRAs are made for each other

$0

Tax on rotations

Every QQQ→TLT and TLT→QQQ rotation inside an IRA is tax-free. No 1099 to file, no short-term capital gains, no wash-sale rules.

T+0

Same-day settlement

In an IRA, proceeds from a sale are immediately available for the next purchase. No good-faith violations, no T+1 wait, no settled-funds restrictions.

2

Funds, not stocks

Only QQQ and TLT — two of the most liquid ETFs on Earth. Available commission-free at every major IRA custodian. No PDT rules apply.

What account types qualify?

Any self-directed account where you can buy QQQ and TLT and execute trades with same-day settlement:

  • Traditional IRA — Tax deferred. Ideal.
  • Roth IRA — Tax-free growth. Even more ideal long-term.
  • SEP-IRA — Self-employed retirement. Works.
  • Solo 401(k) — Self-directed. Works if your provider allows ETF trading.
  • Rollover IRA — Same as Traditional IRA.
  • HSA (Health Savings Account) — Triple tax advantage. Works if invested.

Custodians that work well

Any major brokerage that offers commission-free ETF trading. Confirmed compatible:

  • Fidelity
  • Charles Schwab
  • Vanguard (note: 30-day frequent-trading restriction — may not be ideal)
  • E*TRADE
  • Interactive Brokers
  • TD Ameritrade (now Schwab)
  • Robinhood (note: PDT rule may apply for small accounts)
Note: Vanguard imposes a 30-day frequent-trading restriction on its own mutual funds. ETF rotations may also be subject to similar restrictions in some Vanguard accounts. Check your custodian’s specific policies before subscribing.

A taxable-account warning

If you plan to use Ocean Blue in a taxable brokerage account instead of an IRA, understand the math:

  • Most rotations are short-term gains (held less than 1 year)
  • Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income (up to 37% federal + state)
  • The strategy’s edge over buy-and-hold (about 2pp/year) may be entirely consumed by taxes
  • Wash-sale rules apply if you sell at a loss and rebuy within 30 days

For most subscribers, this means: use it in an IRA, or don’t use it. A taxable-account investor would generally be better served by buy-and-hold QQQ, despite the larger drawdowns.
