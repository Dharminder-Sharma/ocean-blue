Honest about execution

What if you miss a signal?

You will. Eventually. You'll be on vacation, your phone will be dead, you'll see the alert at 11 PM and forget by morning. Here's what that actually costs.

Most signal services pretend execution is perfect. The implication is that you'll see every alert, place every trade, and get fills exactly at the published price. That's not how anyone actually trades, and we'd rather be honest about it.

Here's what late execution actually does to results, based on running the backtest with realistic delay assumptions.

If you execute the same day

This is the published track record — the algorithm signals after Tuesday's close, you place the trade Wednesday morning at the open. CAGR of 16.87%, max drawdown of 27.78%, the numbers on the results page. This is the realistic best-case scenario for a subscriber paying attention.

If you execute one day late

You see the alert that night, but you don't get to a computer until the next evening, so you place the trade two days after the signal instead of one. Over 22 years of backtesting, this costs roughly 0.5 percentage points per year of return. Annualized return drops from about 16.9% to about 16.4%. The max drawdown stays about the same.

For perspective: on a $100K starting portfolio, that 0.5pp/year gap over 22 years compounds to about $200K of difference in ending value. Meaningful, but the strategy is still far ahead of buy-and-hold.

If you execute one week late

You're traveling or distracted, and you don't see the alert until you're back at your desk a week later. Over the backtest, this costs roughly 1.5 to 2 percentage points per year. Annualized return drops to roughly 15%. Drawdowns are slightly worse because you're sometimes holding equities a week into a regime break the algorithm already identified.

Still ahead of buy-and-hold over the long run. Still preserving most of the strategy's edge. The structural pattern holds even with substantial delay.

If you execute a month late

This is the real cost-of-inattention scenario. Over the backtest, monthly delay shaves about 4 to 5 percentage points off the annualized return — down to roughly 12 to 13%. And critically, the drawdown protection mostly disappears. By the time you rotate out of equities a month after the signal fires, the worst of the drawdown has often already happened.

At a month of average delay, the strategy starts to look more like buy-and-hold with extra friction. Most of the edge comes from acting on the regime signal promptly. Rotation is a perishable advantage.

What this means in practice

A few practical conclusions from this analysis:

Same-day execution is achievable for almost everyone. The signal arrives after the close. You have all evening, all night, and until next morning's open to place the trade. SMS delivery (the $5/month add-on) exists specifically to make sure you see the alert promptly.

Being a day or two late is not catastrophic. The strategy is robust to small execution delays. If you're sick, traveling, or otherwise indisposed for a day, you don't need to panic-execute at 3 AM.

Don't go on vacation without a plan. If you're going to be unreachable for a week or more, one option is to set a stop-loss on your QQQ position at the algorithm's current trigger level. That way, if the algorithm signals an exit while you're away, your brokerage executes for you. It's not a perfect substitute, but it preserves most of the protection.

The strategy works best for people who actually pay attention. If you're going to ignore the alerts for weeks at a time, you're better off buying QQQ and holding. The expense of the subscription only makes sense if you're going to act on the signals reasonably promptly.

One more honest note

The 22-year backtest assumes mechanical execution at next-day-open prices, with realistic slippage modeling but no behavioral mistakes. Real subscribers make behavioral mistakes. They override signals they disagree with. They re-enter early. They forget to exit. They take partial positions.

We can't model that, and we won't pretend to. The published results are what mechanical execution of the rules would have produced. Your actual results will be the rules plus your execution discipline. Some subscribers will do better than the published track record because they catch nuances the algorithm misses. Most will do somewhat worse, in line with the universal pattern of individual investors slightly underperforming the strategies they follow.

The point of this page is to make sure you go into the subscription with realistic expectations. Same-day execution: full edge. A day or two late: most of the edge. A month late or skipping signals: probably not worth subscribing in the first place.
